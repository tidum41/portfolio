Checkout redesign usability study

In progress

Twenty-four moderated sessions compared three checkout prototypes for a mid-size apparel retailer. Shoppers completed a realistic purchase with their own shipping details while thinking aloud. The redesign improves clarity on delivery timing and payment errors, but address entry—especially on mobile and for international formats—remains the primary abandonment risk. Participants want stronger autocomplete, visible order totals before payment, and plain-language error recovery when cards fail.

Key insights

Objective 1

Identify where shoppers abandon or hesitate during the redesigned checkout

negative

Shipping address is the highest-friction step, especially on mobile.

Six participants abandoned at address entry or needed moderator rescue. Common issues included unit numbers not mapping to validation rules, international formats clearing the form, and autocomplete selecting the wrong street line.

Objective 2

Measure perceived clarity of delivery options and total cost before pay

positive

Grouped delivery choices with explicit dates reduce second-guessing at payment.

When delivery windows showed calendar dates and tax-inclusive totals on the review step, participants reported higher confidence. Round-three prototype scores on “I know what I will pay” rose 28 points versus round one.

Objective 3

Evaluate error recovery for declined cards and address validation failures

neutral

Payment errors recover when the message explains what to fix; generic declines do not.

Participants retried successfully when copy referenced billing ZIP or card type. Vague “payment failed” banners led to exit without retry in four sessions.

Objective 4

Compare task success across shopper segments (mobile, guest vs. signed-in)

negative

Guest checkout on mobile shows the lowest end-to-end success rate.

Signed-in shoppers benefited from saved addresses; guests on phones were twice as likely to need help on the address step. Segment heatmaps from study chat highlight domestic vs. international gaps.

Popular insights

positive

Progress indicator calms shoppers during longer flows.

A four-step header with labels outperformed a minimal progress bar. Participants knew how much was left and were less likely to bail when delivery options took longer to load.

Apple Pay and Shop Pay shortcuts skip anxiety at payment.

When wallets appeared above manual card fields, eight participants chose them immediately and rated payment as the easiest step.

negative

Hidden fees after address entry erode trust.

Showing shipping only after address validation felt like a bait-and-switch to five participants, even when totals were accurate.

Recommendations

Ship international-friendly address parsing and unit-number fields before the next prototype round.

Keep tax and shipping visible on cart review, not only after address validation.

Replace generic payment failures with field-level guidance and a single primary retry action.

Prioritize guest mobile flows in QA; segment success rates lag desktop by a wide margin.

Add a post-purchase confirmation screen that restates delivery date and support contact.
